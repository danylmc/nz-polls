# Traditional-party combined vote (“purple vote”)

**Compiled 6 October 2026.** Start around **2005** for the main comparison: it captures roughly two decades of change, including countries where the decline happened before 2017. Keep **around 2017** as a second reference point for the recent shift in New Zealand. Each country uses its nearest national lower-house election; where two elections are equally close, the earlier one is used.

These are combined votes for the named traditional parties at **elections**, not current polls. Changes are percentage points. Australia and Ireland use first-preference votes; Germany uses second votes.

**Reading the table.** NZ, Portugal and Denmark show sizeable falls from elections near 2017. Greece, Ireland and Germany lost much of their traditional-party vote earlier; Greece and Spain have since recovered some of it. Canada moved in the opposite direction. The country-by-country election sources are linked in the [data file](data/purple_vote_elections_comparison.csv), with fuller notes in the [election comparison report](reports/purple_vote_election_results.md).

*Portugal's [2015 PSD-led list](https://www.parlamento.pt/sites/EN/Parliament/Paginas/PreviousElectionResults.aspx) and [2025 PSD-led list](https://www.parlamento.pt/sites/EN/Parliament/Paginas/Election-results.aspx) included CDS-PP, so that row is not a pure PS + PSD series. Greece's 2015 PASOK figure also comes from a joint list. Ireland's pair tracks the old governing parties rather than a left/right split; Canada's Liberals are centrist. Denmark's Venstre is a centre-right liberal party despite its name.*

**Netherlands.** PvdA + VVD fell from 35.9% in 2006 to 27.0% in 2017. In 2025, VVD plus the [joint GroenLinks–PvdA list](https://www.kiesraad.nl/actueel/nieuws/2025/11/7/kiesraad-uitslag-tweede-kamerverkiezing-betrouwbaar) also totalled about 27.0%, but this is not comparable with PvdA alone because the joint list includes GroenLinks voters. Including CDA, a third traditional governing party, gives 62.4% (2006), 39.4% (2017) and 38.8% (2025), with the same 2025 caveat. See the [Dutch context data](data/purple_vote_netherlands_context.csv).

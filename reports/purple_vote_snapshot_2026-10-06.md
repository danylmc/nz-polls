# Purple vote: a first peer-country snapshot

For the expanded 2026 analysis using all eligible polls in nine countries, see [`purple_vote_2026_comparison.md`](purple_vote_2026_comparison.md).

**As of 6 October 2026.** Here, “purple vote” means the **sum of the two traditional governing parties’ primary vote or voting intention shares**. It is a measure of how much support remains with those particular parties, not the size of the political centre or the total vote of left and right blocs.

| Country | Traditional pair | Fieldwork end | Red | Blue | Combined |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Canada | Liberal + Conservative | 7 Sep | 49% | 33% | **82%** |
| New Zealand | Labour + National | 6 Oct average | 28.8% | 28.1% | **56.8%** |
| Australia | Labor + Liberal–National Coalition | 27 Sep | 26% | 22.5% | **48.5%** |
| United Kingdom | Labour + Conservative | 2 Oct | 30% | 17% | **47%** |

Sources: [local weighted NZ polling average](weighted_poll_average.csv), [Léger Canada](https://leger360.com/federal-politics-liberals-regain-ground/), [Roy Morgan Australia](https://www.roymorgan.com/), [Opinium UK](https://www.opinium.com/resource-center/voting-intention-30-september-2026/). The latest [1News–Verian poll](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/09/28/poll-greens-surge-to-best-result-ever-as-national-labour-slide-further/) separately has Labour and National at 28% each. The underlying entries are in [`../data/purple_vote_snapshot_2026-10-06.csv`](../data/purple_vote_snapshot_2026-10-06.csv); the chart is [`purple_vote_snapshot_2026-10-06.png`](purple_vote_snapshot_2026-10-06.png).

**Reading:** NZ sits around 8–10 percentage points above the UK and Australia in this snapshot, and 25 points below Canada. The local weighted polling average for 6 October is 56.8% for Labour plus National, close to the 1News poll's 56%.

**Comparability limits:** NZ is a modelled polling average; the peers are single, differently timed polls with different fieldwork and weighting. Canada’s Liberal Party is the traditional centre/centre-left party, not a labour party. Australia reports a *first-preference* sum here: its two-party-preferred figure reallocates preferences and always totals 100%, so it would not measure the same thing. NZ and Australia use proportional/preferential systems while the UK and Canada use first-past-the-post, which can affect incentives to vote for smaller parties. A low purple share can mean support for new parties, enduring minor parties, or independents; it does not by itself establish ideological moderation or polarisation.

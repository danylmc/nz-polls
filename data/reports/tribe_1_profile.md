# Tribe 1: Alienated Conservatives

## Summary

Tribe 1 represents 17.3% of the population (n=344). This group is politically right of centre (mean L-R: 6.4) and predominantly votes for National. Average age is 52 years with 12% identifying as Māori.

## Who They Are

**The informed cynics: older, male, often immigrant, middle-class voters who distrust institutions and are the most cynical about the political system.**

**In class terms**: Lower-middle to middle class. Not wealthy (below-average in top income bracket, fewer outright homeowners) but financially stable. Many are immigrants who came to NZ as adults - only 65% born here, the lowest of any tribe. Their distrust of institutions may reflect immigrant experience with institutions that fail people, or a comparative awareness that NZ institutions underperform their potential.

**Occupationally**: Likely a mix of tradespeople, small business owners, and mid-level public servants. The above-average vocational education suggests practical skills; the slightly elevated "below average income" suggests they're not the winners in the modern economy but get by.

**Why they vote right**: Not ideological free-marketeers (that's Tribe 5). They vote National out of cultural conservatism and a pragmatic judgement that the centre-right is the least-bad option, though their 20% non-vote rate — the highest of any tribe — suggests many have given up on electoral politics entirely.

**The disaffection**: They distrust institutions more than any other tribe and are among the most cynical — they strongly agree that MPs are out of touch (raw mean 1.96/5), that they have no say (1.95), that big interests run government (2.10), and that politicians don't care (1.93). This is comprehensive disillusionment with the political system, which is consistent with their high non-vote rate.

## Interpretation

This tribe shows the lowest trust in institutions of any group — parliament (raw mean 2.93/4), government (2.94), courts (2.62), and police (2.27) all score well above average on the distrust scale (where higher = less trust). They are also the second-most cynical tribe, strongly agreeing that MPs are out of touch (1.96/5), that they have no say (1.95), that big interests run government (2.10), and that politicians don't care (1.93). Only the Precariat Left score as cynical.

This is a coherent profile: they distrust institutions *and* believe the political system is unresponsive. Low trust and high cynicism go together. The high proportion of immigrants (35% not born in NZ, highest of any tribe) may bring comparative perspectives that fuel their institutional disillusionment, and their 20% non-vote rate — second highest after Precariat Left — suggests many have simply disengaged.

On Treaty issues, they oppose removing Treaty references from law (-0.83) and oppose increased Māori say in government (-0.60), a conservative stance that maintains the status quo. They're not culture warriors but nor are they sympathetic to Māori political aspirations.

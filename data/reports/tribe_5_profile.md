# Tribe 5: Establishment Right

## Summary

Tribe 5 represents 17.8% of the population (n=354). This group is politically right of centre (mean L-R: 7.4) and overwhelmingly votes for National and ACT. Average age is 56 years with only 7% identifying as Māori.

**In class terms**: Upper-middle to upper class. The wealthiest tribe by every measure. Over a quarter (27%) are in the top income bracket - nearly double the average. More than half (55%) own their home outright. Almost nobody rents (7% private, 0.7% social). These are the winners.

**The demographic profile is stark**: Older (56), male (57%), Pākehā (79%), married (81%), homeowners (85% own with or without mortgage). This is the "traditional" New Zealand establishment - the people who've accumulated property, superannuation, and financial security over decades.

**Not the educated elite**: Interestingly, university education is slightly below average (16% vs 18%). These aren't academics or professionals who got rich through credentials. They're more likely to have built wealth through business, trades, farming, or property investment. Self-made rather than credentialed.

**Why they're conservative**: Their politics are straightforwardly material. They oppose redistribution because they'd be the ones redistributed from. They oppose government intervention because they don't need government services. They support lower taxes because they pay more taxes. This is rational self-interest, not ideology.

**On Treaty/Māori issues**: Strongly opposed to co-governance (+1.08), Māori representation in government (+0.81), and skeptical of Treaty settlements. With only 7% Māori membership, this tribe has the least personal stake in these issues. Their opposition may be principled (one person, one vote) or self-interested (protecting existing power structures), or both.

## Interpretation

This is the ideological right: 64% National, 17% ACT, and a mean left-right score of 7.4 (the highest of any tribe). Their attitudes are consistently conservative across economic and social domains.

Economically, they oppose redistribution (+1.07 on "government shouldn't reduce differences"), disagree that income inequality is too large (+1.07), oppose higher taxes on high incomes (+1.00), and reject the idea that child poverty reflects societal failure (+0.94). They want more freedom for business (+0.87).

On Treaty issues, they oppose co-governance across all domains (+0.99 to +1.09), reject more Māori say in government (+0.81), and likely support removing Treaty references from legislation.

Yet they're not institutional skeptics - trust scores are near average (+0.05). They don't distrust the system; they believe the system works and has worked for them. Their conservatism is about preservation, not disruption.

**The National-ACT base**: This tribe provides the ideological core of the centre-right. They're not swing voters - they're committed. The 64/17 National/ACT split suggests most prefer the establishment party, but a significant minority want a harder-edged economic liberalism. ACT's growth depends on activating more of this tribe.

**The stakes are clear**: This tribe has the most to lose from left-wing policies (higher taxes, redistribution, Treaty settlements that affect property rights). Their politics are defensive - protecting accumulated advantages. Whether this is described as "protecting what they've earned" or "pulling up the ladder" depends on one's perspective.

# Tribe 2: Educated Progressives

## Summary

Tribe 2 represents 20.6% of the population (n=410). This group is politically left of centre (mean L-R: 2.6) and predominantly votes for Labour and Green. Average age is 47 years with 18% identifying as Māori.

## Who They Are

**The educated liberal class: university-educated, high-income, partnered professionals whose progressivism is ideological rather than material.**

**In class terms**: Upper-middle class professionals. The highest university education rate, lowest vocational education, fewest reporting below-average income, and among the most likely to be in the top income bracket. These are doctors, lawyers, academics, senior public servants, NGO executives, and corporate professionals.

**The demographic profile is striking**: Overwhelmingly NZ-born Pākehā (77% NZ European, 80% born in NZ), highly educated, secular, and partnered. They're the most "settled" tribe economically - stable relationships, good incomes, university credentials.

**Why they're progressive**: Their progressivism is ideological, not material. They don't need government services (they can afford private healthcare, good schools, nice neighbourhoods). They support redistribution, climate action, and Māori rights because they believe these are *right*, not because they personally benefit. This is the politics of values rather than interests.

**The "champagne socialist" critique applies here** - but that's descriptive, not necessarily pejorative. They can afford to care about abstract issues because their material needs are met. Their political engagement is high (highest efficacy scores) because they have the education, time, and confidence to participate.

**Secular liberals**: The second-lowest religious attendance. Their progressive values come from education and cosmopolitan exposure, not religious tradition.

## Interpretation

This tribe is well-educated, firmly left-wing, and — perhaps surprisingly — relatively trusting of institutions. Their trust z-score of -0.34 (where lower = more trust) makes them the second-most trusting tribe after the Middle New Zealand. They also score highest on political efficacy (+0.42, disagreeing most strongly with cynical statements like "a person like me has no say" and "MPs are out of touch"). Trust and efficacy go together for this tribe: they believe the system works and feel personally capable of influencing it.

This makes sense for educated professionals who benefit from how institutions operate — universities, courts, public services — and who have the resources, confidence, and social capital to navigate political systems. They oppose stiffer sentences, support Treaty settlements, back co-governance, and champion women's representation — the full progressive agenda.

The split between Labour (39%) and Green (32%) reflects a divide between pragmatic progressives and ideological ones. Both camps are over-represented here because this tribe has the education and security to vote on values rather than economic self-interest.

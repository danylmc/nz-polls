# Tribe 4: Middle New Zealand

## Summary

Tribe 4 represents 26.3% of the population (n=523) - the largest tribe. This group is politically centre-right (mean L-R: 5.8) and splits between National and Labour. Average age is 53 years with 12% identifying as Māori.

**In class terms**: Solidly middle class. Average on almost every economic indicator - income, education, home ownership. Not wealthy, not struggling. This is the median New Zealander, statistically speaking.

**Demographically unremarkable**: They're average age, average income, average education, average religiosity. The only standouts are being more female (58%) and more likely to own their home outright (38%). This is the suburban homeowner demographic - stable, established, and mildly conservative.

**The largest tribe for a reason**: With 26% of the population, this is where most "normal" voters end up. They're not animated by ideology, identity, or strong opinions. They vote based on performance, vibes, and whether things feel like they're going well.

**Their trust**: High institutional trust (-0.39 on the distrust scale, meaning less distrust than average) is their defining feature. They trust parliament, government, courts, and media more than any other tribe. They also score above average on efficacy (+0.31), disagreeing with statements like "politics is too complicated" and "a person like me has no say." Trust and efficacy go together here: they believe the system works and feel part of it.

**Not culture warriors**: Moderate on social issues (near zero on social_cultural domain). They're not especially progressive or conservative on Treaty, abortion, or social values. They're pragmatic rather than principled.

## Interpretation

This tribe's defining characteristic is institutional trust without strong political engagement. They trust parliament (-0.49 on the distrust scale), government (-0.51), courts (-0.50), media (-0.42), and even social media (-0.40) more than average — all negative z-scores meaning less distrust than the population mean. They trust National (-0.47) and Labour (-0.37) roughly equally.

They also feel personally capable — they score above average on efficacy items (+0.31), disagreeing that politics is "too complicated" (+0.32) and that "a person like me has no say" (+0.27). Trust and efficacy go together: these are people who believe the system works and feel part of it.

The 39% National / 23% Labour split suggests these are classic swing voters. They went National in 2023 likely because of perceived performance rather than ideology — their institutional trust makes them receptive to whoever appears competent. In different circumstances (a failing National government), many would swing back.

**The swing voter tribe**: This is the group elections are fought over. Not animated by ideology, identity, or strong values — they vote on performance and perceived competence. Their trust in institutions makes them responsive to mainstream political messaging, and their sense that the system broadly works means they don't feel urgency to change it — they follow political trends rather than drive them.

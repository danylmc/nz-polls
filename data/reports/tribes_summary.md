# New Zealand Voter Tribes: Summary

This document summarizes the five voter "tribes" identified through cluster analysis of the New Zealand Election Study (NZES) 2023 data, plus supplementary findings from Māori-specific and temporal analyses.

---

## Overview

The analysis applied k-means clustering to 40 political attitude variables from the NZES 2023 dataset (n=1,989), identifying **five distinct voter tribes** that represent the New Zealand electorate.

| Tribe | Name | Size | Left-Right | Top Party |
|-------|------|------|------------|-----------|
| 1 | Trusting Traditionalists | 17.3% | 6.4 (centre-right) | National (38%) |
| 2 | Engaged Progressives | 20.6% | 2.6 (left) | Labour (39%), Green (32%) |
| 3 | Treaty Advocates | 18.0% | 3.8 (left) | Labour (37%), Green (16%) |
| 4 | Skeptical Mainstream | 26.3% | 5.8 (centre) | National (39%), Labour (23%) |
| 5 | Economic Individualists | 17.8% | 7.4 (right) | National (64%), ACT (17%) |

---

## The Five Tribes

### Tribe 1: Trusting Traditionalists (17.3%)

**Archetype**: Older, male, often immigrant, middle-class voters who trust institutions despite modest means.

**Demographics**:
- Average age: 51.5 years
- 54.5% male
- Only 65.2% born in NZ (most immigrant tribe)
- Lower-middle class income profile

**Defining attitudes**:
- Highest institutional trust across all tribes (parliament, government, courts)
- Low political efficacy
- Opposition to removing Treaty references
- Pragmatic acceptance of Māori representation

**Party support**: National (38%)

---

### Tribe 2: Engaged Progressives (20.6%)

**Archetype**: University-educated, high-income, partnered professionals whose progressivism is ideological rather than material.

**Demographics**:
- Average age: 46.5 years
- 55.8% female
- 76.6% NZ European (most Pākehā tribe)
- Highest university education: 22.3%
- Fewest below-average income: 4.1%
- Upper-middle class professionals

**Defining attitudes**:
- Strong support for women's representation, Treaty settlements, co-governance
- High political efficacy despite low institutional trust
- "Champagne socialists" who can afford to vote on values

**Party support**: Labour (39%), Green (32%)

---

### Tribe 3: Treaty Advocates (18.0%)

**Archetype**: Young, female, Māori and Pacific, renters and social housing tenants whose politics reflect material need.

**Demographics**:
- Average age: 42 years (youngest tribe)
- 57.4% female
- 37.9% Māori (2.3x over-represented)
- 9.6% Pacific (3.5x over-represented)
- Lowest home ownership: 11.1% own outright
- Highest social housing: 11.1%
- Highest below-average income: 17.8%

**Defining attitudes**:
- Strong support for Māori representation and co-governance
- Politics rooted in material need, not abstract ideology
- Belief that Treaty settlements haven't gone too far

**Party support**: Labour (37%), Green (16%), Te Pāti Māori (7%)

---

### Tribe 4: Skeptical Mainstream (26.3%)

**Archetype**: The silent majority - older, female, middle-class suburbanites who distrust institutions but aren't ideological.

**Demographics**:
- Average age: 53.2 years
- 58.1% female (most female tribe)
- 72.5% NZ European
- Average on almost every indicator
- Solidly middle class

**Defining attitudes**:
- Institutional distrust without ideological content
- Not animated by ideology or identity
- Vote based on performance and "vibes"
- Classic swing voters

**Party support**: National (39%), Labour (23%)

**Electoral significance**: The election-deciding tribe. Their swing toward National determined the 2023 outcome.

---

### Tribe 5: Economic Individualists (17.8%)

**Archetype**: The propertied class - older, male, wealthy, partnered Pākehā homeowners whose conservatism reflects material success.

**Demographics**:
- Average age: 56.1 years (oldest tribe)
- 57.2% male (most male tribe)
- 78.7% NZ European
- Only 6.8% Māori (half the national average)
- 27% in top income bracket (vs 16% overall)
- 54.8% own home outright (vs 34% overall)
- 81.3% have partner (highest)

**Defining attitudes**:
- Oppose co-governance across all domains
- Oppose redistribution
- Question systemic causes of child poverty
- Rational self-interest protecting accumulated advantages

**Party support**: National (64%), ACT (17%)

---

## Class Structure of NZ Politics

| Class Position | Tribe | % Population |
|----------------|-------|--------------|
| Working class / precarious | Treaty Advocates | 18% |
| Lower-middle class | Trusting Traditionalists | 17% |
| Middle class | Skeptical Mainstream | 26% |
| Upper-middle class (professional) | Engaged Progressives | 21% |
| Upper class (propertied) | Economic Individualists | 18% |

**Key insight**: The left splits by class - Engaged Progressives are wealthy professionals voting on values, while Treaty Advocates are working-class voters voting on material need. The right also splits - Trusting Traditionalists are lower-middle class with institutional faith, Economic Individualists are upper class with self-interest.

## Māori Voter Tribes (k=2 Analysis)

When analyzing the Māori subsample separately (n=695), two distinct tribes emerge:

### Moderate Māori (40%)
- Left-Right: 6.0 (centre-right)
- Top parties: Non-vote (32%), National (26%), Labour (14%)
- Demographics: 59% male, average age 47
- Oppose co-governance, believe Treaty settlements have gone too far
- More politically disengaged

### Progressive Māori (60%)
- Left-Right: 3.9 (left)
- Top parties: Labour (37%), Non-vote (19%), Te Pāti Māori (14%), Green (13%)
- Demographics: 57.5% female, average age 45
- Support co-governance, believe Treaty settlements appropriate
- More politically engaged

---

## Temporal Stability (2017-2023)

The tribe structure remains remarkably stable across elections:
- Cluster correlations: >0.999 between year pairs
- Structure persists despite major events (COVID-19, government changes)

**Tribe size shifts (2017 → 2020 → 2023)**:
- Economic Individualists: 17% → 25% → 28%
- Engaged Progressives: 22% → 30% → 18%
- Treaty Advocates: 13% → 14% → 21%
- Trusting Traditionalists: 32% → 17% → 19%
- Skeptical Mainstream: 16% → 14% → 14%

---

## Demographic Polarisation

### Education Gap
- University graduates are 0.7-1.0 points more left on the 0-10 scale
- 2023: University 4.68 vs Non-university 5.63 (gap: -0.95)
- Gap widened between 2020 and 2023

### Gender Gap
- Men 0.2-0.6 points more right than women
- 2023: Male 5.51 vs Female 5.18 (gap: +0.33)
- Peak gap during COVID-19 (2020: +0.59)

### Age Gap (Largest demographic divide)
- Under 30: 4.49 on left-right scale
- 60+: 5.85 on left-right scale
- Gap of 1.36 points - the largest demographic predictor

## Electoral Implications

1. **Election deciders**: Tribe 4 (Skeptical Mainstream) - the 26% swing voters whose movement determines outcomes

2. **Left coalition challenge**: Must unite Tribe 2 (ideological progressives) and Tribe 3 (material-need voters) despite different motivations

3. **Right coalition base**: Tribe 1 (institutional trust) + Tribe 5 (material self-interest) form National's core support

4. **Tribe stability**: Voters shift between tribes slowly; underlying structure persists across elections
